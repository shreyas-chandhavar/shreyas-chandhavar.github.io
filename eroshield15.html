EROSHIELD-15

Leading-Edge Protection & Aerodynamic Trade Study

A preliminary engineering study of a separately controlled leading-edge protection concept for the outer region of the public IEA 15-MW reference blade. The work links SolidWorks CAD, differential BEM screening, numerical verification, mass and attachment calculations, span-placement decisions and TurboPLM configuration control.

Engineering problem

Protection itself has an aerodynamic cost.

Leading-edge erosion can reduce aerodynamic efficiency in the high-speed outer blade region, but a protection system is not free: thickness and termination geometry can introduce their own lift and drag penalties.

The project therefore evolved from a simple “recover erosion loss” concept into a trade study that treats erosion severity and protection penalty independently.

Final design insight: protection can be beneficial under moderate or severe erosion while becoming counterproductive under mild erosion when the protection penalty is too high.

Engineering decision

Keep the current span. Reject false validation.

The station-specific span-placement study found that the existing 9.551 m CAD location captured approximately 99.98% of the maximum screened recovery among the tested fixed-length candidates. Moving it changed recovered power by only about 0.034 kW, so the existing CAD span was retained.

An early SU2 CFD branch produced nonphysical force behavior, including negative drag. Those results were rejected and excluded from quantitative claims rather than used as decorative “validation.”

Claim boundary

Closed at preliminary design / screening level.

EROSHIELD-15 is not a validated commercial LEP product. The erosion anchors and LEP analogue penalties come from different airfoils and Reynolds numbers and are transferred only as engineering sensitivity inputs. The actual 2 mm shield geometry has not been validated aerodynamically.

The study does not include geometry-specific wind-tunnel validation, final adhesive peel/fracture qualification, structural FEA of the final attachment, rain-erosion qualification, thermal/environmental ageing, blade-flexure compatibility or full power-curve/AEP integration.

The proposed Phase-12 feathered/scarf termination redesign was intentionally stopped before completion. The released Rev-A geometry therefore remains the final controlled CAD baseline.
